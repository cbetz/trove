What is IRS Form 990 Schedule H?

The community-benefit schedule that nonprofit hospitals attach to their annual tax return.

Schedule H is a schedule of IRS Form 990, the annual information return tax-exempt organizations file with the IRS. An organization that operates one or more hospital facilities files a Schedule H with its return, reporting the financial assistance and other community benefit it provided that tax year, along with bad debt, Medicare and facility information. Returns filed electronically are public, and the IRS publishes them in bulk.

Where Schedule H comes from

Tax-exempt hospitals do not pay federal income tax and can issue tax-exempt bonds, and they are expected to provide benefit to their communities. Schedule H was introduced with the redesigned Form 990 for tax year 2008 as the place where that benefit is reported in a standard form. The Affordable Care Act in 2010 added section 501(r) of the tax code: a community health needs assessment (CHNA) every three years, a written financial assistance policy, limits on charges to patients eligible for financial assistance, and limits on extraordinary collection actions. Schedule H asks about each.

Structure of Schedule H

Schedule H has six parts; the last is narrative:

  • Part I — Financial Assistance and Certain Other Community Benefits at Cost. The main table. Line 7 has a row for financial assistance (7a), Medicaid (7b), other means-tested government programs (7c), community health improvement (7e), health professions education (7f), subsidized health services (7g), research (7h) and cash and in-kind contributions (7i), with totals on 7d, 7j and 7k. Each row has columns for (a) number of activities, (b) persons served, (c) total community benefit expense, (d) direct offsetting revenue, (e) net community benefit expense, which is (c) − (d), and (f) percent of total expense.
  • Part II — Community Building Activities. Housing, economic development, environmental improvements, workforce development and similar activities.
  • Part III — Bad Debt, Medicare, and Collection Practices. Bad debt expense, Medicare revenue and allowable cost, and the collection policy.
  • Part IV — Management Companies and Joint Ventures.
  • Part V — Facility Information. One section per hospital facility: CHNA, financial assistance policy, billing and collections.
  • Part VI — Supplemental Information. Narrative explanations for lines in the other parts.

Line 7a: financial assistance at cost

Line 7a is the cost of free or discounted care provided under the hospital's financial assistance policy. It has three dollar columns: (c) total expense, (d) direct offsetting revenue, and (e) net, which is (c) − (d). Trove publishes all three.

Under the Schedule H instructions (Worksheet 1), a filer enters Medicaid or provider taxes in the line 7a cost when payments from an uncompensated care pool or DSH program in its state are intended primarily to offset the cost of financial assistance, and reports the pool revenue as offsetting revenue in column (d). When the payments are primarily to offset the cost of Medicaid services, both go to line 7b instead; when state law does not make the purpose clear, the instructions allow a proportional split. This is a classification the instructions permit, and it depends on the state. The worksheet itself is not part of the public return.

Where the data lives

The IRS publishes Form 990 e-file submissions as XML, in ZIP files grouped by the year of release. A hospital's tax year 2022 return (a fiscal period that began in 2022) can be in the 2023, 2024, 2025 or 2026 release, depending on when it was filed. Trove's tax year 2022 ingest opens every ZIP in those four releases, 51 in all, and reads each return's own tax year, without relying on the IRS index files to locate returns. It found 2,360 returns with a Schedule H: 849 in the 2023 release, 1,464 in 2024, 47 in 2025 and none in 2026.

More than one return can exist for one EIN and tax year: an amended return, or a short-year return next to a full-year one. Trove's Schedule H file keeps every return found. Where one return per EIN is needed, trove takes a full-year return over a short-year one, then the latest period end, then the latest e-file timestamp, so an amended return is used over the original.

Line 7a next to CMS Worksheet S-10 line 23

Hospitals also report a charity-care figure to CMS, on their Medicare cost report: Worksheet S-10, line 23, column 3, the cost of charity care after patient payments. Trove puts the two public figures side by side for every hospital it can match. They are not two measurements of one thing:

  • Provider taxes and state pool payments. Line 7a can include provider taxes in cost, with pool revenue in column (d), under the condition above. The cost report keeps Medicaid DSH and supplemental payments, "net of associated provider taxes or assessments", on S-10 line 5, which is not part of line 23.
  • Insured patients. S-10 line 23 column 2 counts deductibles and coinsurance written off for insured patients.
  • Costing method. Schedule H asks for the filer's most accurate costing methodology (a cost accounting system, a cost-to-charge ratio, or another method). S-10 derives cost from charges with the worksheet's own cost-to-charge ratio.
  • Reporting unit. A 990 is filed by a legal entity (an EIN), a cost report by a provider (a CCN). One EIN can cover several CCNs.
  • Fiscal period. CMS groups cost reports into fiscal-year files by the date the reporting period begins. Trove takes, for each provider, the cost report whose period ends closest to the end of the 990's tax period, and marks a row period-aligned when the two end within one month.

The two lines are defined differently, so they can differ when both are filed correctly. A difference is a reason to open both filings and read the components; it does not, by itself, say anything about either filing. The hospitals page walks through one hospital.

How to use Schedule H data

  • Query trove's published Parquet file with DuckDB over HTTPS. It has one row per tax year 2022 return with a Schedule H (2,360 returns, 2,295 EINs): 
    SELECT organization_name, tax_period_end,
       financial_assistance_at_cost_gross          AS line_7a_c,
       financial_assistance_at_cost_offset_revenue AS line_7a_d,
       financial_assistance_at_cost                AS line_7a_e
FROM read_parquet(
  'https://troveproject.com/data/schedule_h_2022.parquet')
WHERE ein = '060646652';
    The hospitals page shows line 7a next to the cost report figure for 2,099 matched nonprofit hospital systems, 1,904 of which have both figures.
  • Install the hcris-analyst Claude Code skill to ask questions in plain language about a specific hospital.
  • Download the XML from the IRS via irs.gov. The trove repository (github.com/cbetz/trove) has the parser.

Columns in trove's Schedule H file

  • Identity and period: ein, organization_name, filer_city, filer_state, tax_period_begin, tax_period_end, tax_year, return_version.
  • Provenance: object_id (the IRS e-file object id, which identifies the exact return), return_ts (e-file timestamp), amended_return, release_year, batch_id (the ZIP it was found in).
  • Form 990 totals: total_revenue, total_expenses.
  • Part I line 7, rows 7a to 7k: for each row, column (e) under the row's name (for example financial_assistance_at_cost, unreimbursed_medicaid, total_community_benefit), column (c) with the suffix _gross, and column (d) with the suffix _offset_revenue.
  • Other: total_community_benefit_pct (line 7k column (f), as a fraction), bad_debt_expense (Part III), hospital_facility_count (Part V).

A blank cell on the return is missing in the file; a reported 0 is 0. The full field list is in the Schedule H field reference on GitHub.
