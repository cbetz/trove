What is an FDA approval package?

The approval letter, the label and FDA's review documents: what each one is and where to find it.

Updated November 26, 2026.

An FDA approval package is the set of documents FDA releases after it approves a drug: the approval letter, the approved label, and FDA's own reviews of the application. FDA posts them on Drugs@FDA for products approved by its Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER). The reviews are the most detailed public record of why a drug was approved.

For any of the 217 novel approvals in trove's index, the drug page links each document directly. Example: Filsuvez has its label, approval letter, review documents and Drug Trials Snapshot one click away.

What is in an approval package

DocumentWho writes itWhat it tells you
Approval letterFDA, to the sponsorThe approved indication, the agreed labeling, postmarketing requirements and commitments, any REMS, and the FDA contact for the application
Label (prescribing information)The sponsor, approved by FDAIndications, dosing, warnings, adverse reactions, use in specific populations, clinical studies
Summary reviewFDA division or office leadershipThe decision and the reasoning, across disciplines, in one document
Clinical (medical) reviewFDA medical officerThe trials, the endpoints, efficacy and safety findings, the reviewer's recommendation
Statistical reviewFDA statisticianTrial design, analysis of the primary and secondary endpoints, subgroups, missing data
Clinical pharmacology reviewFDA clinical pharmacologistPharmacokinetics, dose selection, drug interactions
Nonclinical (pharmacology/toxicology) reviewFDA pharmacologistAnimal and laboratory studies
Chemistry (product quality) reviewFDA chemistsManufacturing, formulation, stability

FDA's own short definitions, from the Drugs@FDA glossary: an approval letter is "an official communication from FDA to a new drug application (NDA) sponsor that allows the commercial marketing of the product", and a review is "the basis of FDA's decision to approve an application ... a comprehensive analysis of clinical trial data and other information prepared by FDA drug application reviewers." Newer packages often combine several disciplines into one document titled Multi-Discipline Review or Integrated Review.

The label is the sponsor's document, approved by FDA. The reviews are FDA's documents. The label says what the drug is approved for; the reviews say why FDA agreed, and where reviewers had reservations.

What is the Summary Basis of Approval?

"Summary basis of approval" (SBA) is the name FDA's disclosure regulation, 21 CFR 314.430, uses for the agency's summary of the safety and effectiveness data behind an approval. People still say "SBA" for the approval package as a whole. On current CDER packages you will not usually find a document with that title: the equivalent is the Summary Review, with the discipline reviews behind it. For cell and gene therapies and other CBER products, FDA publishes a Summary Basis for Regulatory Action (SBRA) on the product's page.

What is in an FDA approval letter?

An approval letter is a few pages long. It names the application and the product, states that the application is approved and for which use, refers to the agreed labeling, and lists what the sponsor must do after approval: postmarketing requirements and commitments, any required pediatric studies, and any Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS). A drug approved under accelerated approval has its confirmatory trial requirement in the letter. It closes by naming the FDA regulatory project manager to call with questions about the application, and is signed by an FDA division or office director.

How to find an approval package on Drugs@FDA

  1. Open Drugs@FDA and search by drug name or application number.
  2. On the application's page, expand "Approval Date(s) and History, Letters, Labels, Reviews".
  3. In the "Original Approvals or Tentative Approvals" table, the row for submission ORIG-1 links the Label, the Letter and the Review. "Review" opens a table of contents with every review document as a PDF.

The application page has a stable address: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=215064, with the six-digit NDA or BLA number at the end. Document addresses usually follow these shapes, though FDA does not guarantee them (corrected letters and combined applications are named differently):

label    .../drugsatfda_docs/label/2023/215064s000lbl.pdf
letter   .../drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2023/215064Orig1s000ltr.pdf
reviews  .../drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/215064Orig1s000TOC.html

Reviews are posted some time after approval, so a recent approval can have a letter and label but no review link yet. When trove read Drugs@FDA in May 2026, 191 of the 192 CDER approvals in its 2021–2024 index had an approval letter posted and 190 had review documents.

Approval packages for cell and gene therapies (CBER)

Products licensed by the Center for Biologics Evaluation and Research are not in Drugs@FDA. Each has its own page under fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics with the package insert, approval letter, Summary Basis for Regulatory Action and review memos. trove links that page for the 25 CBER products in its index, for example Casgevy.

What an approval package does not contain

  • Patient-level trial data. Reviews summarize and re-analyze the trials; the data sets are not released. Trial registrations and results are on ClinicalTrials.gov.
  • Anything after approval. Later label changes and new indications are separate supplements, each with its own letter, listed lower on the Drugs@FDA page.
  • Unredacted text. Trade secrets and confidential commercial information are blacked out.
