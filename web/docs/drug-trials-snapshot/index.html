What is an FDA Drug Trials Snapshot?

FDA's short public summary of who took part in the trials behind a new drug approval.

Updated November 27, 2026.

A Drug Trials Snapshot is a short FDA page that shows who took part in the clinical trials that supported a new drug's approval, broken down by sex, race, age and ethnicity. In FDA's words, Snapshots "provide consumers and healthcare professionals with concise information about who participated in the key clinical trials that supported the original FDA approval of new drugs". They also show where the trials were run and whether benefits or side effects differed among demographic groups.

trove links the Snapshot from the drug page of each approval that has one: 235 of the 331 novel approvals in its 2021–2026 index. Examples: Filsuvez, Miplyffa, Tzield, Lamzede, Rivfloza.

Which drugs have a Drug Trials Snapshot?

FDA publishes Snapshots only for new molecular entities and original biologics approved by its Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER). The program began in 2015. Generics, new formulations and new indications of older drugs do not get one, and neither do products approved by a different FDA center, CBER, such as cell and gene therapies. See what a new molecular entity is.

What a Snapshot contains

  • What the drug is for and how it is used, in plain language.
  • Who was in the trials: the number of participants and their breakdown by sex, race, age group and ethnicity, usually as charts.
  • Where the trials were conducted.
  • Benefits and side effects, and whether either differed by sex, race or age.
  • A "MORE INFO" section with the technical detail: trial designs and the data tables behind the charts.
  • A link to the prescribing information at the top and bottom of the page.

Each Snapshot is written as questions and answers, consumer information first.

When is a Snapshot published, and is it updated?

FDA says Snapshots "are published within 30 days of approval and contain information that was available at the time of approval." They are not updated afterwards. A Snapshot describes the trials behind the original approval, so it does not cover indications added later.

Limits of a Snapshot

  • It covers the key trials behind the original approval, not every study of the drug.
  • FDA cautions that conclusions about differences among demographic groups "cannot always be made", often because a subgroup is too small.
  • It is a summary. The full analysis is in FDA's review documents; see what is in an FDA approval package.
  • FDA says not to rely on Snapshots alone to make decisions about medical care.

How to find a Drug Trials Snapshot

  • FDA lists every Snapshot, newest first, on its Drug Trials Snapshots page, along with yearly summary reports.
  • FDA's annual Novel Drug Approvals lists link each drug's Snapshot next to its approved use.
  • On trove, search FDA drug approvals for the drug; its page links the Snapshot along with the label, approval letter and review documents.
