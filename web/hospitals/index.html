Hospital charity care: the IRS figure and the CMS figure, side by side

Nonprofit hospitals publish a financial-assistance figure on their IRS Form 990 and a charity-care figure on their Medicare cost report. This page puts the two public figures next to each other for every hospital we can match, with the components from each filing that show what is in the difference.

The two figures, for tax year 2022:

  • IRS Form 990, Schedule H, Part I, line 7a reports financial assistance at cost: column (c) total expense, column (d) direct offsetting revenue, and column (e) net, which is (c) − (d).
  • CMS cost report (form 2552-10), Worksheet S-10, line 23, column 3 is the cost of charity care after patient payments: uninsured patients (column 1) plus insured patients (column 2).

The two lines are defined differently, so they can differ even when both are filed correctly. The IRS instructions let a filer include Medicaid or provider taxes in the line 7a cost when payments from its state's uncompensated care pool or DSH program are intended primarily to offset the cost of financial assistance, with that pool revenue reported as offsetting revenue; otherwise those amounts belong on line 7b. The cost report keeps Medicaid DSH and supplemental payments, net of provider taxes, on a separate line (S-10 line 5). Line 5 holds the payments not already included in line 2 (Medicaid revenue); neither line is part of line 23. The S-10 insured column counts deductibles and coinsurance written off.

A difference between the two is a reason to open both filings and read the components. It does not, by itself, say anything about either filing. How to read a difference walks through one hospital.

Of the $77.5M difference, about $71.5M appears to be Connecticut's hospital provider tax net of the state supplemental payments it funds: the amount on S-10 line 5, which the cost report keeps separate from charity care. The worksheet behind the IRS line is not public, so this is a reconciliation from the hospital's own filings, not a figure the hospital has stated.

The return's own Schedule H narrative (Part III, line 4) describes the program. It says the Connecticut Disproportionate Share Hospital Program “was established to provide funds to hospitals for the provision of uncompensated care” and is funded in part by an assessment on hospital revenue, and that in the year ended September 30, 2023 the system paid $317.9M into the program and received $198.2M in distributions. Across the system's four Connecticut hospitals in this data, line 7a column (d) sums to $198.2M and S-10 line 5 sums to −$119.7M, which is the difference between those two amounts. The narrative does not say what is in line 7a, so this remains a reconciliation.

Method and definitions

What is in the ranked index, and what is left out

The lookup holds every EIN matched across the CMS cost report files, IRS Schedule H and the CCN-to-EIN crosswalk: 2,099 systems, of which 1,904 have both figures. The ranked index is the subset where the two lines can be set side by side without a unit or period mismatch. A row is in the index when all four hold:

  • One hospital. One CCN with a cost report under the EIN, so the 990 and the cost report describe the same facility. For 39 index rows the crosswalk lists further CCNs that have no cost report in the files used; those rows carry a badge.
  • Same fiscal period. The cost report's period ends within one month of the 990's.
  • Both figures at least $1M.
  • Same scale. Total expenses on the two filings are within 5% of each other.

The funnel: 2,099 matched, 1,904 with both figures, 1,797 of those period-aligned, 1,642 of those with a single cost report, 493 in the index, in 48 states.

Multi-hospital systems are in the lookup but not in the index. Mayo Clinic's return covers 19 cost reports under one EIN. Cleveland Clinic's covers 17, one of which has no S-10 line 23, so no system total is computed for it and the partial sum is shown separately, labelled as partial.

The difference is always the CMS figure minus the IRS figure. The percentage is the absolute difference as a share of the larger of the two. The ranked index is also on GitHub as a readable table, with the full funnel in the method summary.

What each line measures, and why the two can differ when both are filed correctly
  • IRS Schedule H, Part I, line 7a is financial assistance at cost: column (c) total community benefit expense, column (d) direct offsetting revenue, column (e) net. Under the Schedule H instructions (Worksheet 1), a filer enters Medicaid or provider taxes in the line 7a cost when payments from an uncompensated care pool or DSH program in its state are intended primarily to offset the cost of financial assistance, and reports the pool revenue as offsetting revenue. When the payments are primarily to offset the cost of Medicaid services, both go to line 7b instead; when state law does not make the purpose clear, the instructions allow a proportional split. This is a classification the instructions permit, and it depends on the state.
  • CMS Worksheet S-10, line 23 is the cost of charity care after patient payments, for uninsured patients (column 1) and insured patients (column 2), with the total in column 3. Medicaid DSH and supplemental payments, “net of associated provider taxes or assessments”, are on line 5 of the same worksheet. Line 5 holds the payments not already included in line 2 (Medicaid revenue); neither line is part of line 23.
  • The insured column. S-10 column 2 counts deductibles and coinsurance written off for insured patients. A hospital whose IRS figure is close to its S-10 uninsured column will show a difference about the size of column 2.
  • Costing method. Schedule H asks for the filer's most accurate costing methodology (a cost accounting system, a cost-to-charge ratio, or another method). S-10 derives cost from charges with the worksheet's own cost-to-charge ratio.
  • Reporting unit. A 990 is filed by a legal entity (an EIN), a cost report by a provider (a CCN). One EIN can cover several CCNs; the index keeps only one-to-one rows, and the lookup shows sums across cost reports where there are several.
  • Status. Cost reports can be as submitted, amended, settled or reopened, and a 990 can be amended. Each detail card shows the cost report's status and whether the return is an amended one. Figures can change in later CMS files.
Missing is not zero

A line with no value on the form is shown as “not reported on this line”. It is never shown as $0, and no difference is computed for that row. 159 matched systems have no S-10 line 23 figure and 53 have a blank line 7a column (e).

Examples: The Johns Hopkins Hospital files a Worksheet S-10 with no value on line 23. St. Jude Children's Research Hospital and Texas Children's Hospital have no Worksheet S-10 in the CMS file. All three are in the lookup with their IRS figure and no difference.

How fiscal periods are matched

CMS groups cost reports into fiscal-year files by the date the reporting period begins. We load the fiscal year 2022 and 2023 files and, for each CCN, take the cost report whose period ends closest to the end of the hospital's tax year 2022 Form 990. A row counts as period-aligned when the two end dates are within one month; for a system with several cost reports, every one of them has to be. 1,797 of the 1,904 systems with both figures are aligned. The detail card shows both periods and says when they are not aligned; a difference on a row that is not aligned compares two different years.

Home-county Social Vulnerability Index

Each detail card shows a Social Vulnerability Index value for the hospital's home county, from the CDC/ATSDR 2022 SVI release (overall summary rank RPL_THEMES, as a national percentile). A higher percentile means a more vulnerable county. This is a proxy, not a service-area measure: a hospital's patients can come from well beyond its home county. Source: CDC/ATSDR SVI.

Coverage and known limits
  • Sources. CMS HCRIS hospital cost report files (form 2552-10) for fiscal years 2022 and 2023; CMS extract generated 2026-10-02, posted 2026-10-05. IRS Form 990 e-file releases 2023 through 2026, returns for tax year 2022.
  • Crosswalk. CCNs are linked to EINs with the Community Benefit Insight crosswalk (December 2024), which lists 2,385 EINs. For-profit and government hospitals file no Form 990 Schedule H and are not here. Some CCNs in the crosswalk have no cost report in the two CMS files.
  • Checked by hand. The 25 rows at the top of the index were checked against the CMS cost report file and the XML of the return. Corrections are welcome at github.com/cbetz/trove/issues.
  • Filing links. The IRS link on each card opens that return's Schedule H on ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer. The CMS links open the provider's record in CMS's Hospital Provider Cost Report data on data.cms.gov, a summary dataset CMS derives from the same cost reports; its “Cost of Charity Care” field is S-10 line 23 column 3. CMS refreshes that dataset on its own schedule, so a figure there can differ slightly from the file used here.
