FDA compilation of CDER NME and new biologic approvals, 1985–2025, as CSV

Every new molecular entity and new biologic approved by FDA's CDER since 1985, in formats you can load directly.

This is FDA's Compilation of CDER NME and New Biologic Approvals, 1985–2025, as CSV and Parquet. FDA publishes the compilation as an Excel workbook only. The files here hold the same 1,387 approvals, one row each, with ISO dates and application numbers that match Drugs@FDA.

What is in it

FDA describes the compilation as a listing of all new molecular entities approved since 1985 and regulated by the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research, covering both small-molecule drugs approved under an NDA and new biological products approved under a BLA. Product information, indications and regulatory characteristics reflect each application at the time of original marketing approval. The file here has 1,387 approvals from 1985 through 2025; FDA's source file is 2026 Compilation_of_CDER_NME_and_New_Biologic_Approvals_1985-2025.xlsx, dated May 12, 2026.

It does not include vaccines, blood products, cell and gene therapies or other products approved by CBER, and it does not include generics, biosimilars or supplements to existing applications.

What trove changed, and what it did not

  • Format. Excel to CSV and Parquet. Column names are lower-case with underscores.
  • Dates. FDA's workbook stores older dates as Excel serial numbers and newer ones as text such as 12/23/2025. Both are written as ISO dates (2025-12-23).
  • Application numbers. Zero-padded to six digits (19010 becomes 019010) so they join to Drugs@FDA and to the per-drug index.
  • One added column. days_receipt_to_approval.
  • Everything else is FDA's text as written, including qualified values such as "Yes (indication [A] only)" and "Priority (used priority review voucher)". Leading and trailing spaces are trimmed. Rows are sorted by approval date.

FDA's disclaimer applies to these files too: the dataset is for research purposes, some fields are simplified, and it reflects each application at the time of initial approval. For the current label and approval history of a product, use Drugs@FDA. FDA takes corrections to the compilation at the address on its page.
