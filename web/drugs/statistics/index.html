FDA drug approval statistics, 1985–2025

Novel drug approvals per year, review designations and review times, computed from FDA's own compilation.

FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) approved 1,387 novel drugs from 1985 through 2025: 1,108 under new drug applications (NDAs) and 279 under biologics license applications (BLAs). That is an average of 33.8 a year. The most in one year was 59 (1996 and 2018); the fewest was 18 (2007). In 2025 CDER approved 46.

2026 is not in FDA's compilation yet, so it is not in these figures. FDA's running list showed 57 CDER novel approvals in 2026 as of November 24, 2026. See the 2026 list to date.

"Novel" here means new molecular entities and new therapeutic biologics approved by CDER, as listed in FDA's Compilation of CDER NME and New Biologic Approvals (file dated May 12, 2026). Every figure on this page is a count over that file; the CSV is one click away if you want to check one.

How many novel drugs does the FDA approve each year?

From 2016 through 2025, CDER approved an average of 46.6 novel drugs a year, ranging from 22 to 59. Over the full 1985–2025 record the average is 33.8 a year.

How long does FDA take to approve a novel drug?

For the 466 novel drugs CDER approved from 2016 through 2025, the median time from FDA's receipt of the application to approval was 298 days (about 9.8 months). It was 242 days for the 295 applications with priority review and 365 days for the 171 with standard review.

FDA defines the receipt date as the date "the first and complete marketing application was received by FDA". The span is calendar days from that date to original approval.

How many novel approvals use priority review, orphan designation or an expedited program?

Of the 466 novel drugs approved from 2016 through 2025, 295 (63%) had priority review, 236 (51%) had orphan drug designation, 178 (38%) had fast track designation, 141 (30%) had breakthrough therapy designation and 84 (18%) were accelerated approvals. One drug can have several of these.

How many drugs has the FDA approved in total?

This page counts novel approvals only: 1,387 new molecular entities and new biologics approved by CDER since 1985. It is not a count of every drug product FDA has approved. Generics, biosimilars, new formulations, new indications and products licensed by CBER (vaccines, blood products, cell and gene therapies) are outside it; Drugs@FDA lists all CDER-approved products.

How to read these numbers

  • State at original approval. FDA's compilation records each application as it stood when first approved. Indications, designations and withdrawals after that date are not reflected.
  • Programs have start dates. FDA lists accelerated approval as not applicable before April 15, 1992, fast track before November 21, 1997, and breakthrough therapy before July 9, 2012. Counts for years that straddle a start date cover only the approvals after it.
  • Priority review before 1992. The priority and standard labels date from the 1992 user-fee law. For 1985–1991 FDA maps its older A/B/C ratings onto them: types A and B count as priority, type C as standard.
  • Orphan designation means the sponsor held orphan designation for at least one of the indications approved at original approval.
  • CDER only. Vaccines, blood products, and cell and gene therapies are licensed by CBER and are not in these counts. The per-drug index lists CBER cell and gene therapy approvals for the years it covers.

Get the data

Download fda_nme_compilation.csv or fda_nme_compilation.parquet, or read the column definitions. The year table above reproduces with one query:

SELECT approval_year, count(*) AS approvals,
       count(*) FILTER (WHERE review_designation LIKE 'Priority%') AS priority,
       median(days_receipt_to_approval) AS median_days
FROM 'https://troveproject.com/data/fda_nme_compilation.parquet'
GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1 DESC;
