# Hospital charity care on two public filings: the ranked index — TY2022

Nonprofit hospitals publish a financial-assistance figure on their IRS Form 990 and a charity-care figure on their Medicare cost report. This table puts the two public figures next to each other for every hospital that can be matched to a single cost report for the same fiscal period, with the components from each filing that show what is in the difference.

- **IRS Form 990, Schedule H, Part I, line 7a** is financial assistance at cost: column (c) total community benefit expense, column (d) direct offsetting revenue, column (e) net = (c) − (d).
- **CMS cost report (form 2552-10), Worksheet S-10, line 23, column 3** is the cost of charity care after patient payments: uninsured patients (column 1) plus insured patients (column 2).

**The two lines are defined differently, so they can differ even when both are filed correctly.** The IRS Schedule H instructions (Worksheet 1, line 4) let a filer include Medicaid or provider taxes in the line 7a cost when payments from its state's uncompensated care pool or DSH program are intended primarily to offset the cost of financial assistance, with that pool revenue reported as offsetting revenue (Worksheet 1, line 6); otherwise those amounts belong on line 7b. The cost report keeps Medicaid DSH and supplemental payments, net of provider taxes, on a separate line (S-10 line 5) that is not part of line 23. The S-10 insured column counts deductibles and coinsurance written off.

The table is the part of the full [matched dataset](community_benefit_gap_2022.csv) where one 990 can be set against one cost report for the same period. Each hospital below is:

- a **single reporting facility in the cost-report data** (the EIN has exactly one CCN with a cost report in the HCRIS files used, so nothing is summed across facilities);
- **period-aligned** (the cost report's period ends within 1 month of the 990's tax period — the two filings cover the same year);
- **material** (S-10 line 23 col 3 and Schedule H line 7a col (e) are both at least $1M); and
- reporting **the same-scale entity** (the two forms' total expenses agree within 5%).

**493 hospitals in 48 states** meet all four conditions (funnel: 2,099 matched systems → 1,904 with both figures present → 1,797 period-aligned → 493 in this index). The S-10 figure is the larger one for 321, the Schedule H figure for 169, and 3 show the same amount. For **107** of the 493 the two figures differ by more than 50% of the larger one.

**† 39 rows are marked †.** For those, the CCN↔EIN crosswalk (December 2024 vintage) lists more than one CCN under the EIN, but only one of them has a cost report in the FY2022 or FY2023 HCRIS files, so the row compares that one cost report with the EIN's 990, and the total-expense test above still has to pass. Across all matched EINs the crosswalk lists 245 CCNs that have no cost report in the FY2022 or FY2023 file; none of them appears in the FY2024 file either, which is consistent with retired or merged provider numbers. The JSON carries both counts (`ccns_with_cost_report`, `crosswalk_ccn_count`).

## The columns

- **7a (c) gross** — Schedule H Part I line 7a column (c): total community benefit expense for financial assistance at cost. Under the Schedule H instructions (Worksheet 1, line 4) a filer includes Medicaid or provider taxes here when payments from the state's uncompensated care pool or DSH program are intended primarily to offset the cost of financial assistance.
- **7a (d) offset** — column (d): direct offsetting revenue. Under Worksheet 1 (lines 6 and 7) this is revenue from uncompensated care pools or programs plus other direct offsetting revenue. The return does not itemise it.
- **7a (e) net** — column (e) = (c) − (d). This is the IRS figure in the comparison.
- **S-10 L23** — Worksheet S-10 line 23 column 3: cost of charity care after patient payments. This is the CMS figure in the comparison. Columns 1 (uninsured) and 2 (insured) are in the JSON and CSV.
- **S-10 L5** — Worksheet S-10 line 5: Medicaid DSH and supplemental payments not already in line 2, *net of associated provider taxes or assessments*. It is negative when the taxes netted against it exceed the payments. It is not part of S-10 line 23. The Schedule H instructions (Worksheet 1, line 4) let a filer include provider taxes in line 7a cost under a stated condition, so where line 5 is negative it is worth comparing with the difference.
- **Net diff** — S-10 L23 − 7a (e). **Gross diff** — S-10 L23 − 7a (c). **%** — |net diff| as a share of the larger of the two net figures.

> A difference does not by itself show that either filing is incorrect. The two lines are defined differently and the forms give filers choices. Each row links to the exact Schedule H used, so the figures can be checked against the return. A blank cell means the form has no value on that line; it is never a reported $0.

## Notes in the JSON

Each row of [`charity_care_gap_index_2022.json`](../web/data/charity_care_gap_index_2022.json) has a `notes` list of fixed codes. They are facts read off the filings and the crosswalk, not judgements:

- `irs_7a_inconsistent` (1 row) — on the return itself, Schedule H line 7a (c) - (d) differs from (e) by more than $1,000 (a blank (d) is read as 0).
- `irs_offset_reported` (143 rows) — Schedule H line 7a col (d), direct offsetting revenue, is greater than 0.
- `cms_line5_reported` (301 rows) — Worksheet S-10 line 5 has a value (positive or negative).
- `multi_ccn_crosswalk` (39 rows) — the crosswalk lists more than one CCN for this EIN; only one has a cost report in the HCRIS files used.
- `cms_report_as_submitted` (103 rows) — the cost report's status is as submitted (not yet settled, amended or reopened).
- `irs_amended_return` (12 rows) — the 990 used is marked as an amended return.

## Why the two figures can differ

These are differences in what the two lines are defined to hold. None of them means a filing is incorrect.

- **Provider taxes and pool payments.** The Schedule H instructions (Worksheet 1, line 4) let a filer put Medicaid or provider taxes in line 7a column (c) when its state's uncompensated care pool or DSH payments are intended primarily to offset the cost of financial assistance, and report the pool revenue in column (d); otherwise both go to line 7b, and where state law does not make the purpose clear the instructions allow a proportional split. The cost report puts Medicaid DSH and supplemental payments, net of provider taxes, on S-10 line 5, outside line 23. Compare the S-10 L5 column with the difference.
- **Offsetting revenue.** Schedule H line 7a column (e) is net of direct offsetting revenue (column d); S-10 line 23 is net only of patient payments. The gross difference column shows the comparison before that offset.
- **Insured patients.** S-10 line 23 column 2 counts deductibles and coinsurance written off for insured patients. A hospital whose IRS figure is close to its S-10 uninsured column (column 1) will show a difference about the size of column 2.
- **Costing method.** S-10 applies the cost-to-charge ratio from the cost report; the Schedule H instructions let a filer use the ratio from their Worksheet 2 or a cost accounting method it considers more accurate.

## A worked example

Yale New Haven Hospital (EIN 06-0646652, CCN 070022, fiscal year ending 2023-09-30), from the two filings:

| Line | Amount |
|---|--:|
| IRS Schedule H line 7a, column (c) | $257,262,512 |
| IRS Schedule H line 7a, column (d) | $144,162,000 |
| IRS Schedule H line 7a, column (e) | $113,100,512 |
| CMS S-10 line 23, column 3 | $35,568,680 |
| CMS S-10 line 5 | −$71,469,000 |
| Difference between (e) and line 23 | $77,531,832 |

Of the $77.5M difference, about $71.5M appears to be Connecticut's hospital provider tax net of the state supplemental payments it funds: the amount on S-10 line 5, which the cost report keeps separate from charity care. The worksheet behind the IRS line is not public, so this is a reconciliation from the hospital's own filings, not a figure the hospital has stated.

The other hospitals of the same system in the data show the same pattern: Greenwich Hospital (line 5 −$19.84M, difference −$19.58M); Lawrence + Memorial Hospital (line 5 −$16.32M, difference −$16.31M); Bridgeport Hospital (line 5 −$12.08M, difference −$13.16M).

The return's own Schedule H narrative (Part III, line 4) says the system paid $317.9M into Connecticut's DSH program assessment and received $198.2M in distributions that year. Across the system's four Connecticut hospitals in this data, line 7a column (d) sums to $198.2M and S-10 line 5 sums to −$119.7M, the difference between those two amounts. This is still a reconciliation: the return does not say what is in line 7a.

A reading of the two filings behind each of the 25 largest differences, sorted by what the published components show, is on the [hospitals page](https://troveproject.com/hospitals/#how-to-read). For some of them neither filing shows a mechanism; those are open questions.

## Method & reproduction

Generated by [`scripts/build_gap_index.py`](../scripts/build_gap_index.py) from [`community_benefit_gap_2022.csv`](community_benefit_gap_2022.csv). The full dataset (2,099 matched systems, 1,904 computable) and its caveats are documented in [`community_benefit_gap_2022_summary.md`](community_benefit_gap_2022_summary.md). CMS HCRIS Hospital 2552-10 FY2022 and FY2023 files, CMS extract generated 2026-10-02, posted 2026-10-05, latest cost-report processing date 2026-09-30 (each cost report matched to the 990's own period) × IRS 990 Schedule H TY2022 (e-file releases 2023–2026). US-government-work source data, public domain.

```bash
uv run python scripts/build_gap_dataset.py   # builds the full gap CSV
uv run python scripts/build_gap_index.py     # builds this index
```
